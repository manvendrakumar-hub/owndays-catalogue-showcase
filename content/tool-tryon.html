Product try-on video

Tool already built Runs locally, not deployed Many PIDs: planned, not run at scale

A chain of Python scripts turns one PID into a catalogue video of a model wearing the product on a plain studio wall. The product on screen comes from the product's own photos and nowhere else, so the frame in the video is the frame in the photos. The tool that drives the chain is already built: a small interface where a teammate picks PIDs, a person and a motion, reviews the prompt and the cost quote, generates, and reads the check result, for one PID or a whole list.

What was done to make it

  1. PhotosOnly the two that matterThe 3/4 view and the side view are picked and checked. A wrong-angle photo makes the model invent the temple, so unsure PIDs go to review.
  2. PromptA fixed template with slotsOnly the person, background, light, outfit and motion change. The product is never described in words.
  3. GenerateThe photos are the productIn the first runs the product was invented. Feeding the real photos as references fixed it, on a woman and on a man, and on a second, very different frame.
  4. CheckA number and a second lookEach clip gets a score and flags, then a visual review. The team's eye outranks the scores.
  5. Deliver4:5 for every videoThe video model makes 3:4, so every clip is cropped to 4:5.

Built with

  • Video modelSeedance 2.5, the model kept: pores, hinge and wordmark read sharply and the frame colour is closest to the photos. Its cost is on the Cost and time tab.
  • Delivery4:5, cropped from 3:4.
  • ClipsThe clips made so far are 6 s. The plan is one 8 s clip of five shots per model: put-on, profile, macro, frontal hold and a close-up pan.
  • ToolBuilt and used locally. It is not deployed yet.
  • Not yetRunning many PIDs. The plan is thousands of short catalogue videos from one automated template, each different in product, person and motion.
