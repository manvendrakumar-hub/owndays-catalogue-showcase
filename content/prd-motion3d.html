What was asked

A short, square product video of an Owndays frame in three dimensions. The frame turns, opens, comes apart into its parts and comes back together, with a few short lines of text that point at what the viewer is looking at: the hinge, the temple, the fit. An existing exploded-view product video was the reference for the motion, and this one is made in Owndays colours and type.

Why we are making it

A photo shows what a frame looks like. Motion shows how it is put together and how it fits: its parts, its hinge, the length of its temple. If the look, the accuracy and the cost per PID hold up for three frames, the same approach can be reused across the catalog.

  • GoalOne exploded-view video per PID.
  • First PIDs233791 (AIR AU2104A-4A, glossy black eyeglasses), 233811 (AIR AU2105A-4A, matte black eyeglasses with grey temple tips) and 254954 (collaboration SR2010M-6A, brown halftone eyeglasses).
  • FormatSquare, 1:1, 15 seconds, no sound.
  • ContentSizes from the product page and short lines of text. The wording is a draft that still needs approval.
  • Product on screenA 3D model in separate parts: built from the product's own photos for the two AIR frames, and the product's real 3D try-on model for the collaboration frame. Nothing is generated by an image model.
  • BrandOwndays guidelines: white ground, black text, Founders Grotesk and the OWNDAYS | AIR mark.
  • StatusMade for 3 PIDs. The latest versions are waiting for review. Running many PIDs is a plan.

What the video shows

  1. OpeningA closed frame rises into viewIt turns as it lands, and the first line of text fades in.
  2. OpensThe temples unfoldThe camera drifts round to a three-quarter view.
  3. HingeClose on the hingeThe camera moves in on the hinge hardware. On the collaboration frame the 3D model has no hinge, so one is rebuilt from the product's images.
  4. TempleA measured side viewThe camera pulls back to a still side view and a thin line measures the temple along its length.
  5. FitFront close-up with the sizesLens width, bridge and temple length from the product page.
  6. PartsThe frame comes apartFront, lenses, nose pads, temples and hinge hardware separate, then come back together.
  7. CloseThe mark and the model numberThe frame closes and the OWNDAYS | AIR mark appears.

What good looks like

  • The product is the product. The hand-built models follow the photos: the front outline matches the traced photo to about 0.15 px on average and the temples to about 1 px (measured). Depth, hinge hardware and nose-pad shapes are not visible in the photos, so they are inferred. The collaboration frame's model comes from the product's real 3D file, and its measuring line reads the listed 140 mm temple (checked in code).
  • The measurement is true. The line along the temple reads within about 1 mm of the listed length on all three frames (measured).
  • The motion is smooth. No sudden jumps, and no text over the product (both checked on every video).
  • It looks like Owndays. A white ground, black text, brand fonts and the real mark, and nothing else.

How the decisions unfolded

  1. 12 OctBuilt from photosNo 3D files were on hand, so each frame was modelled from its traced white-background photos, as separate parts.
  2. 12 OctImage-to-3D tools set asideOne was tested. It fused the thin rims and left out the hinges and nose pads, so it could not be taken apart.
  3. 12 OctOpening matched to the referenceThe closed frame's rise and turn were fitted to the reference video frame by frame.
  4. 12 OctMeasurement redoneA moving camera bent the measuring line. It now draws on a still side view, like the explainer videos.
  5. 12 OctReal 3D files foundA hand-built model is only as exact as its photos. The try-on service on the product pages holds a real 3D model for most frames: 23 of the 26 frames we checked have one. We have used it on one frame so far.
  6. 13 OctTemples attachedIn the first real-model videos the temples floated off the front, because the model has no hinge. A hinge was rebuilt from the product's images, the temples were seated on the end pieces, and the frame now closes fully. The hinge is inferred from images, not taken from the 3D file.
  7. 13 OctA one-command runOne script now takes a PID from its product page to a checked video with no AI model in the loop. It ran end to end for one PID in about 25 minutes, after one script fix on its first try.
