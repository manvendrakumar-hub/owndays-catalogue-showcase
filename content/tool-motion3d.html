Built and used for 3 PIDs Many PIDs: planned, not run at scale

A chain of Python scripts turns the photos of one PID into a 3D model and a finished exploded-view video. It traces each photo into an outline, builds the frame as separate parts, moves the camera and the parts through a fixed sequence, renders every frame, and puts the text, the mark and the measuring line on top. Checks run on the model and on the video before it is delivered. A second route skips the tracing and the modelling: when the product's own 3D try-on model exists, it is downloaded, assembled and used instead. When the model has no hinge, one is rebuilt from the product's images so the temples attach to the front. One script runs the whole chain with no AI model in the loop.

What was done to make it

  1. TraceFive photos become outlinesFront, side, three-quarter, hinge and folded views are cut out and traced. The photo's floor reflection is dropped.
  2. ModelSeparate parts, to scaleEach photo is scaled to the sizes on the product page, then the frame is built as parts that can move apart.
  3. CheckModel against photoThe model's outline is compared with the photo's in pixels. Hidden details such as hinge hardware and depth are inferred.
  4. AnimateA fixed sequenceRise, unfold, hinge, a still side view with a measuring line, the parts coming apart, and a close. Version 4 added the still side view.
  5. RenderAbout 25 to 41 minutes a videoMeasured on one Mac: about 24 minutes for the real-model frame at a lower sample count, and 36 to 47 minutes for the three hand-built frames. This is the slow step.
  6. OverlayText, mark and lineAdded on top in Owndays type and colours, kept inside the brand's safe margin.

Built with

  • 3D and renderBlender 5.1, rendered on the Mac's own GPU.
  • TracingOpenCV and a Python port of potrace.
  • Text and markHyperFrames (HTML scenes to video) with ffmpeg.
  • FontsFounders Grotesk.
  • Output1080 × 1080, 30 fps, H.264, no sound.
  • Proven onThree frames: a glossy black frame and a matte black frame with metal-arm nose pads and grey temple tips (hand-built), and a collaboration frame (real 3D model).
  • Not yetRunning many PIDs. The one-command run is proven on one PID. A person still reviews the video, approves the wording and handles exceptions, such as a PID with no try-on model. At about 25 minutes a video, many PIDs would still need several machines.
