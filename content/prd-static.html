What was asked

A tool that turns an Owndays product number (a PID) into the size image used on the product page: the frame seen from the front and from the side, arrows that mark the lens width, the bridge, the lens height and the temple length, and a column of spec cards. The tool reads the details and the photos from the Owndays site itself, finds the right photos, places the arrows where the frame really is, and checks the result against the brand guidelines before anyone looks at it. A hand-built design in Figma is the pattern to follow.

Why we are making it

A hand-built size image takes a designer's time for every frame, and each one can drift from the guidelines. If a PID can produce its own image, with the numbers taken from the product page and the arrows placed from the photos, the same approach can cover the catalog, and every image arrives with its brand check attached. The finished size images are on the Output tab. The tool's own screens and the pictures of how the two versions differ are on the Tools tab.

What the image shows

  1. FrontThe frame from the frontArrows mark the lens width and the lens height; a third marks the bridge. Each has a call-out with its size in millimetres.
  2. SideThe frame from the sideOne arrow runs the length of the temple, with its call-out.
  3. CodeThe size codeLens width, bridge, temple and lens height in one line, such as 53-16-145-33.
  4. CardsSeven spec cardsSize, gender, shape, rim, frame colour, material and weight.

The brand-guidelines gate: the Brand Bouncer

Every image goes through the Brand Bouncer before it is shown. It checks the real image against the 169-page Owndays Brand Guidelines. It holds 21 rules, each with the guideline page it comes from: 15 are checked automatically on the finished image, 4 are decisions for the brand team, and 2 do not apply to a size chart. The score is the share of the automatic checks that pass. A person's decision is never counted as a pass.

Each PID is made in two versions, side by side, so the difference is visible. Figma design follows the Figma file exactly. Brand accuracy keeps the same photos, measurements and fonts and changes only what a guideline asks for. The Tools tab has a picture for each difference.

Four open questions for the brand review

These are decisions the guidelines leave to the brand team, so the tool leaves them open in both versions. We will go through them on a call.

  1. p.68–69Red on a size imageThe guidelines reserve red for sales and campaign communications. The arrows and call-outs use red. Is that right for measurements, or should they be black?
  2. p.67, p.69White type on redThe call-outs set white text on red. Is that one of the recommended colour combinations?
  3. p.107The 12 × 12 gridThe tool checks that the two blocks sit on grid lines. A designer confirms the layout also reads as on-grid.
  4. p.27, p.167Brand-management reviewThe guidelines ask for every final application to be reviewed by the brand management team. The tool cannot stand in for that.

What good looks like

  • The numbers are the product's. Every millimetre comes from the product page. The photo only decides where the arrows go, and where the photo and the page disagree the tool says so.
  • The arrows land on the frame. On 233791 the lens opening in the photo has a width to height of 1.60 against 1.61 from the listed 53 and 33 (measured), and the bridge measures 16.3 mm against 16 mm listed (measured).
  • The right photos are found without help. On a sample of 71 PIDs from over 20 brands, the front and side photos were found on 68. The frames that were not found are rimless or half-rim, which have no closed lens outline to measure.
  • It follows the guidelines. 100% of the automatic checks for Brand accuracy, with the four human decisions listed, not hidden.
  • Nothing is invented. A value the site does not publish shows a dash and a flag.

How the decisions unfolded

  1. 12 OctDetails and photos from the Owndays siteThe product page carries the four frame sizes, the spec tiles and the photos, so the tool starts from a PID alone.
  2. 12 OctPhotos found by shape, not by positionThe first approach assumed dark frames and failed on thin wire frames. It now works on any frame colour, by the shape of the frame against the white background.
  3. 12 OctFetching does not saveA fetched PID is temporary and clears itself after a day. Only the Save button keeps it, so the tool stays light.
  4. 12 OctTwo versions, one gateThe Figma design scored 87%. Rather than quietly change it, the tool shows both versions and what each guideline changes.
