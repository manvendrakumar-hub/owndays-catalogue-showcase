Product explainer video

Made for 3 PIDs, in 2 languages Running it for many PIDs: planned, not built

What was asked

A short, square product video for an eyewear PID. It explains the product in plain words: its shape, material, size and fit, with smooth motion, on-screen text and a spoken explanation. It is made from the details on the product page and from the product's own white-background photos, cut out so the product sits on a clean ground, and it follows the Owndays brand guidelines.

Why we are making it

Every PID already has the same kind of facts and photos. If one explainer works well for one PID, the same template can explain all of them, in more than one language, without a shoot and without generating any imagery. The first video proved the look, the voice and the cost per PID. The first PID was a plain black frame, so two more PIDs, chosen by hand because they look very different from it, now test how well the tool holds up on other frames. After review, we decide which PIDs to keep.

  • GoalOne clear explainer per PID, in two languages.
  • PIDs233791 (OWNDAYS AIR AU2104A-4A: glossy black, full rim, square), 223715 (OWNDAYS SUN SUN2099T-2S: light brown sunglasses, full rim, Boston) and 254954 (OWNDAYS and POMPOMPURIN SR2010M-6A: brown halftone, full rim, Boston).
  • FormatSquare, 1:1.
  • LanguagesEnglish with a Singapore accent first. Mandarin second.
  • VoiceA female voice in both languages, so the video sounds local to its audience.
  • AudienceCustomers in Asia, mostly in Singapore.
  • ContentProduct details and features only. No prices or offers, and nothing the page or the photo does not show.
  • Product on screenThe real photo with its background removed. Nothing is generated.
  • BrandOwndays guidelines: Founders Grotesk, white ground, black text and logo.
  • StatusMade for 3 PIDs in 2 languages. The first PID is approved; the other two are waiting for review. Running it for many PIDs is a plan.

What the video shows

  2. Scene 2Shape and colourFull rim, the lens shape and the colour. The outline is traced, the lens shape is marked, and a glint crosses the frame.
  3. Scene 3MaterialWhat the frame is made of, with a close-up of one detail: the silver hinge, the gold bridge, or the engraving on the temple.
  4. Scene 4SizeLens width, bridge, lens height and temple length are drawn to scale, with counting numbers.

  6. Scene 6CloseThe brand mark, the frame and the model number.

What good looks like

  • The product is the product. The photo is untouched apart from its background, and the numbers read aloud match the page.
  • It explains, it does not sell. Plain statements from the page facts, no filler and no price.
  • Words land on the voice. Each on-screen line appears as it is spoken.
  • It looks like Owndays. Brand fonts, a white ground, the black logo and nothing else.
  • The arrows land on the frame, whatever the frame. The lens width and height measured off each photo agree with the sizes on the page to within 1% on all three frames (0.5% and 0.3% on the two new ones), so the drawing is to scale. The numbers read out are always the page's.
  • The tool says where the photo and the page disagree. On both new frames, the gap between the lenses in the photo is about 2 mm wider than the bridge on the page (22.6 mm against 20, and 21.3 mm against 19). The arrow follows the photo and the number is the page's. On the first PID the two agree within 0.3 mm.

Open points for the review

  • The collaboration. The Pompompurin video follows the product page: the name and the engraving are shown as they are on the product. Only the Owndays Air lockup exists as artwork, so the second name (SUN, POMPOMPURIN) is set in Founders Grotesk beside the real OWNDAYS wordmark. The brand team should confirm this is acceptable.
  • Mandarin wording. The Mandarin lines for the two new PIDs have not been read by a native speaker. The terms to check are 波士顿形 (Boston), 醋酸纤维 (acetate), 棕色渐变 (brown halftone) and 浅棕色 (light brown).
  • How the voice says the names. "Pom Pom Purin" and "Boston" need a listen in both languages.
  • Colour words. The page calls 223715 "Light Brown". In the photos it reads as a darker tortoise brown. The video uses the page's words.
